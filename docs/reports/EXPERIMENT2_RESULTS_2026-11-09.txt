EXPERIMENT 2 RESULTS -- JOINT vs SEQUENTIAL PLANNING ON THE TESTBED
Prepared November 9, 2026. Plans: generated_plans/ALNS24_joint.json and
generated_plans/ALNS24_seq.json (24 bays, 6 robots, capacity 2, 30 s
processing, 2 s drop). Design, order and stopping rule fixed November 2,
2026, before any counted run (docs/CHANGES_2026-10-30.txt).

KEY RESULTS
- Joint planning finished faster in 7 of 8 pairs.
- Mean makespan: joint 341.8 s, sequential 364.2 s.
- Paired difference (sequential minus joint): mean 22.4 s, median 15.8 s.
  Sequential runs took 6.6% longer on average.
- Hodges-Lehmann shift: 16.0 s, about 96% CI [4.5, 48.6] s.
- Paired t-test (primary analysis in thesis Section 4.6.4): t(7) = -2.47,
  p = 0.043; mean difference (I - S) -22.4 s, 95% CI -43.8 to -1.0 s.
- Exact Wilcoxon signed-rank, two-sided (check): W = 1, p = 0.0156 (the
  smallest p possible with 8 pairs is 0.0078). The differences are not
  normal (Shapiro-Wilk p = 0.010, pair 5), so the check matters.
- Sign test, as a check: 7 of 8, p = 0.070.
- The planned makespans were almost equal (joint 306.98 s, sequential
  306.07 s, from the plan files). The gap appears in EXECUTION: joint runs
  overran their plan by 34.8 s on average (11.3%), sequential runs by
  58.1 s (19.0%).
- The dashboard and the metrics database show 290.98 s and 290.07 s: the
  page leaves out the 2 s drop DWELL at each of the critical robot's 8
  visits. The plan-file values are the right planned makespans.
- No counted run had a collision. All 16 completed every job.

FAILED ATTEMPTS (not counted; each rerun in its slot, per the protocol)
- Sequential: 1 of 9 attempts failed (S7, November 3, 16:50). Alvik3
  drifted toward Alvik5 in bay 1; the deadlock sidestep ended in a
  reported 0.03 in overlap.
- Joint: 2 of 10 attempts failed (J12 at 09:08 and J14 at 09:21, November
  9). Both had the same cause at bay 9 (Alvik1 and Alvik6): an early yield
  return, a stale reservation hold, and an off-route exclusion.
- Attempt success rate: joint 8/10 (80%), sequential 8/9 (89%).

SECONDARY MEASURES (per run, means over the 8 counted runs)
- Deadlocks detected: joint 6 in total, sequential 8. All were resolved in
  counted runs.
- Fleet traffic delay (robot-seconds): joint 726.8, sequential 641.2.
  Joint robots spent MORE time waiting in traffic, yet finished sooner.
- Near misses and collisions: 0 in every counted run.

ROBUSTNESS
- Without pair 5 (the sequential run with Alvik3's 53 s guard-hold delay
  at bay 1): 6 of 7 favor joint, mean -14.1 s (95% CI -24.0 to -4.1),
  t-test p = 0.014, exact Wilcoxon p = 0.031. The result does not rest
  on that one pair.
- Day: the four Nov 3 differences (+11.0 to +30.1) and the four Nov 9
  differences (-6.3 to +80.8) both favor joint. Pairs were never split
  across days.
- Order: joint ran first in pairs 1, 4, 6 and 8 (differences +30.1, +16.4,
  -6.3, +15.3) and second in 2, 3, 5 and 7 (+11.0, +16.7, +80.8, +15.3).
  No order effect is visible.

LIMITATIONS
- One plan per method. This shows that THESE two plans differ in execution.
  The method-level claim rests on the ALNS screens over three seeds.
- Rerun-until-success. A run that failed was redone in its slot, so the
  counted joint runs are the ones that avoided the bay-9 recovery bug. Joint
  failed more often (2 attempts) than sequential (1), so this selection, if
  anything, flatters joint. Report the attempt success rates next to the
  makespans.
- Software: one supervisor version for all 16 counted runs (md5
  a8954d443ef3111806ab07f11c1c0aa1). Known recovery bugs are logged and
  deliberately NOT fixed during collection.
- Recurring execution events: Alvik3's leftward drift at bay 1 hit the
  sequential plan twice (one failure, one 53 s delay). It is tied to that
  plan's routing past an occupied bay, so it is partly a plan effect and
  partly a robot effect.

INTERPRETATION
The two plans are equally good on paper (within 1 s), but the jointly
planned one ran 16-22 s faster on the real fleet in 7 of 8 matched pairs.
The difference is significant under the test declared before collection.
Joint plans finished sooner while accumulating more total traffic waiting.
This suggests that planning routes together with the assignment keeps the
critical path clear even when individual robots wait more. Breaking the
makespan down per robot along the critical path would test that reading.
The failure counts argue for reporting reliability alongside speed: on this
testbed, joint was faster but failed one more attempt, both times through
the same supervisor recovery bug, not through a planning conflict.
