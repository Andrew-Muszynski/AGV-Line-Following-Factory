# Testbed work queue after the National Guard demo

Written 2026-09-30 from the supervisor log covering three clean runs plus one
run on a different algorithm that produced a physical collision. Nothing here
is speculative — every item names the log evidence it came from.

Ordered by what actually broke a run, not by how interesting it is.

---

## What happened in the collision run

The sequence, reconstructed from timestamps:

```
1790779474.14  Alvik4  leg 18->19: driving from (23.9,36.9) to (33.5,36.8)
1790779475.34  EMERGENCY STOP ['Alvik3','Alvik4']:
                 closing at 4.3in/s with 6.47in left (needs 6.55in to stop)
1790779477.75  Alvik3  turn at 123 toward 123->74: staged turn -84.8deg
1790779491.45  Alvik4  leg 19->27: no progress for 5s
                 (stuck at 11.98in from target, last status 'BUSY WHEEL_FOLLOW_MODE')
```

Node 123 is a **workstation entry**; Alvik4 was driving to node 19, a lattice
node next to it. Three separate defects stacked:

1. The guard fired **0.08in too late** — it needed 6.55in of stopping distance
   and had 6.47in.
2. Alvik3 was **stationary, so not "closing"** — then it rotated, and the
   rotation swept into Alvik4. The guard does not model a rotating robot's
   swept footprint at all.
3. Alvik4, stopped and nudged, **never resumed** and failed the leg 16s later.

That last one is what you saw as "stalled in place while the other robots
continued."

---

## 1. The emergency brake triggers too late — HIGHEST PRIORITY

All four events this session, with the margin:

| closing | gap left | needed to stop | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.6 in/s | 5.93 in | 5.93 in | exactly at the limit |
| 3.6 in/s | 6.12 in | 6.29 in | **0.17in too late** |
| 4.3 in/s | 6.47 in | 6.55 in | **0.08in too late** |
| 6.0 in/s | 5.99 in | 7.14 in | **1.15in too late** |

**Three of four fired when stopping was already impossible.** The guard is
computing the right number and acting on it one moment too late — it triggers
*at* the threshold instead of with a margin ahead of it.

Fix: trigger at `needed_to_stop * k` for some k > 1, or add a fixed lead. The
number to pick comes from the worst case above (1.15in short at 6.0 in/s), so
a lead of ~2in or k≈1.3 would have caught all four. Measure rather than guess
— the stopping model itself may also be optimistic.

**Alvik3 is in all four events.** Worth checking whether that is a routing
coincidence or a robot that brakes worse than the others.

## 2. Rotation sweep is not modelled

A robot parked at a workstation entry is *stationary*, so no closing rate is
computed for it — but when it rotates, its corners sweep well outside its
resting footprint, and an entry node sits mid-edge with lattice nodes close on
both sides.

This is what actually made contact in the collision above. The proximity check
needs to treat "neighbour is about to rotate / is rotating" as an expansion of
its occupied radius, not as a stationary point. The firmware already announces
rotations, and `camera_grid_navigate` logs `staged turn from X to Y` before it
starts — the information exists; nothing consumes it.

## 3. A robot stopped by the guard never resumes

Six of seven stalls this session ended with `last status 'IDLE'`:

```
1 stuck at 11.98in from target, last status 'BUSY WHEEL_FOLLOW_MODE'
1 stuck at 15.09in ... 'IDLE'
1 stuck at  6.79in ... 'IDLE'
1 stuck at  6.69in ... 'IDLE'
1 stuck at  5.68in ... 'IDLE'
1 stuck at  2.64in ... 'IDLE'
1 stuck at  2.48in ... 'IDLE'
```

STOP exits `STATE_WHEEL_FOLLOW` and leaves the robot IDLE. Nothing re-arms it,
so the leg sits there until `no progress for 5s` aborts it. **That is the whole
"vision leg failed → retry" pattern** — the retries are a symptom of this, not
an independent problem.

Two of them were only ~2.5in short of the target, which is a robot that had
essentially arrived and gave up.

Fix: after an emergency stop or a brake that leaves the robot short, re-arm
`WHEEL_FOLLOW_MODE` and finish the leg instead of waiting to time out.

## 4. Trace exactly where a vision leg fails  *(operator request)*

`vision leg failed (drive_leg failed on leg A->B)` is one message for several
distinct causes. Observed this session:

| underlying cause | how it reads now |
|---|---|
| stopped short, went IDLE | `no progress for 5s ... 'IDLE'` |
| blocked while still driving | `no progress for 5s ... 'BUSY WHEEL_FOLLOW_MODE'` |
| mode re-arm ack lost | `did not ack WHEEL_FOLLOW_MODE before turn` |
| settle could not converge | `SETTLE FAILED -- -6.27deg off 270 after 4 passes` |
| position disagreed with plan | `off-route — measured (...) vs expected node N` |

Add a machine-readable failure reason to the event itself — a short enum
(`STALLED_IDLE`, `STALLED_DRIVING`, `MODE_ACK_LOST`, `SETTLE_FAILED`,
`OFF_ROUTE`) carried from `drive_leg` up through the supervisor's event, so the
Decision Log and the metrics DB can count them by cause instead of needing a
log dig every time. That is the difference between "three legs failed" and
"three legs failed, all of them stopped-short-and-IDLE".

## 5. Startup guards  *(operator request)*

### 5a. Supervisor should wait for live robot data

Cost a debugging session on 2026-09-30: the agent was restarted while the
supervisor kept running, which orphaned every subscription. Six robots read
OFFLINE with no explanation, PING did nothing, green LEDs everywhere.

`run_role_linux.sh supervisor` waits for rosbridge's port but not for robot
traffic. Make it block until poses are actually arriving and print
`waiting for robots (N/6 publishing)...`, so this failure states itself.

**Do not verify with `ros2 topic echo --once` per robot.** Each invocation is
a fresh node that must redo DDS discovery; run three times in a row it blamed
Alvik4, then Alvik1+Alvik4, then Alvik3, while all six were publishing at
19.6-20.0 Hz. `tools/check_robots_live.py` is the correct check — one node,
one discovery round. `ros2 topic info` is also unreliable here: it reported
`Publisher count: 1` for all six while nothing was flowing, and 0 and 1 for
the same topic minutes apart.

### 5b. Vision should retry its rosbridge connection

`apriltag_localize.py` checks `is_connected` once, prints
`rosbridge: not connected, poses not being published`, and then silently never
publishes again. So restarting rosbridge or the camera bridge silently takes
vision down. Make it reconnect.

## 6. Alvik1 does not reliably stop rotating  *(hardware)*

Eight `STOP NOT verified stationary` on Alvik1 in the 2026-09-30 morning run,
none on any other robot. Position held to 0.01in while yaw swept ~21deg after
four STOP commands — the robot kept turning. Led to 12-27deg coasts and
eventually to driving off the taped grid onto bare table.

The settle amplifier is already fixed (`settle_floor_update()`, 2026-09-30),
so a missed stop now degrades instead of diverging. The underlying robot
fault is untouched: check Alvik1's drivetrain against another robot by hand.

## 7. Off-route detection fires but nothing acts on it

```
8x  off-route — measured (-0.01,1.99) vs expected node 9  (0.99 cells)
8x  off-route — measured (1.04,2.25) vs expected node 26  (0.76 cells)
```

Two events, each re-logged 8 times. Being a full cell off-route is a real
localization/plan disagreement, and repeating the same warning 8 times is a
gate holding with no resolution path — the same shape as the vision-corridor

# Run of 2026-10-01: four findings

## A. Rotation accuracy is now PER-ROBOT, not a global tuning problem

134 staged turns. **65.7% finish outside the 2deg requirement**, 11.9%
outside 10deg. But it is not uniform:

| robot | n | median \|err\| | p90 | max | >5deg | final10/commanded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alvik3 | 26 | **1.58** | 2.69 | 4.58 | **0%** | **1.32x** |
| Alvik2 | 24 | 2.25 | 8.51 | 15.74 | 21% | 1.96x |
| Alvik4 | 14 | 2.35 | 4.96 | 5.43 | 7% | 1.60x |
| Alvik5 | 23 | 2.85 | 6.18 | 9.50 | 22% | 2.42x |
| Alvik6 | 22 | 4.04 | 13.83 | 19.04 | 45% | 3.62x |
| Alvik1 | 25 | **7.18** | 21.71 | 24.77 | **56%** | 1.99x |

`final10/commanded` is the measured rate over the last 10 samples against
the rate the ladder was commanding. It predicts the error:

* turns finishing inside 2deg: median **1.18x**
* turns finishing outside 10deg: median **2.54x**

The stages do not track -- they are coast-downs, which
`--staged-approach-kp`'s help text already documents (25 RPM stage
achieving 141deg/s against 45 commanded). What is NEW is that the amount
of overshoot varies almost 3x between robots, so a single global constant
cannot hold 2deg on all six. Alvik3 already meets the requirement on every
turn with the current settings.

**Proposal: calibrate per robot, as the firmware already does for
ROTATE_CARRIER_GAIN.** Derive a per-robot handoff2 (or a per-robot rate
scale) from logged `final10 vs commanded` pairs. Do NOT re-open the global
taper -- `--staged-approach-kp` is 0.0 deliberately after it regressed.

**Alvik1 is a separate fault, not a calibration point.** Its ratio (1.99x)
is mid-pack while its error is by far the worst, which is the signature of
the unresolved `STOP NOT verified` defect (it keeps rotating after STOP).
Exclude it from any fit until its drivetrain is checked.

**On 'too slow':** the crawl begins at ~44deg remaining on a 90deg turn,
because `--staged-handoff2-deg` defaults to 45. That is deliberate and
measured (25/30/35/40/45 gave worst-case 5.7/2.9/3.5/1.9/1.65deg), so
lowering it trades accuracy for speed. A per-robot value is the way to buy
speed on the robots that can hold it -- Alvik3 at 1.32x has margin that
Alvik6 at 3.62x does not.

## B. Alvik4: banked position did not reach the off-route gate

```
vision leg failed (SETTLE_FAILED: turn toward next leg failed at 55)
[Alvik4] drive to 55 had completed; banking it so the retry is not refused as off-route
[Alvik4] SUPERVISOR WAIT (vision: off-route - measured (5.99,5.99) vs expected node 47 (0.99 cells))
   ... x100 ...
[Alvik4] off-route on 100 consecutive checks; excluding
```

Cell (5.99,5.99) IS node 55; node 47 is cell (6,5). The supervisor logged
that it banked the completed drive, then the off-route gate went on
comparing against node 47 for ~60 seconds until the robot was excluded.
So the bank did not update what that gate reads. Two bugs in one: the
reconciliation does not take, and the gate's only escape is exclusion.

Note the failure reason came through as `SETTLE_FAILED` -- the taxonomy
added 2026-09-30 working as intended.

## C + D. Both depot-lane departures began with a FLEET-WIDE POSE STALL

```
alvik5: turn at 0 toward 0->DE1: lost vision mid-turn -- aborting route
alvik3: turn at DE2 toward DE2->DE3: no fresh vision -- aborting route
alvik3: [STALLED_DRIVING] leg DE1->DE2: no progress for 5s (stuck 14.71in from target)
alvik3: leg DE1->DE2: lost encoder + camera-assisted odometry -- aborting route
```

Alvik5 drove a wheel off the table edge; Alvik3 steered off the line and
lost its position. **Neither is a steering-tuning problem -- both lost
their pose first and then drove blind.** The common location is the depot
lane and node 0, the bottom edge of the camera frame.

That makes this the priority of the four: it is the only one that can put
a robot on the floor. Worth measuring before tuning anything:

1. Per-tag detection rate **as a function of table position** -- the fleet
   statistics are aggregates and would hide an edge-of-frame hole.
2. Whether robots in the lane occlude each other's tags at that angle.
3. What the navigator does on vision loss mid-turn: aborting the route
   leaves the robot wherever it stopped, which in the lane is next to an
   edge.

A hard geometric bound would help regardless of the cause: refuse to drive
when the commanded target is outside the known table extent, so a bad pose
cannot produce a path off the edge.

### ROOT-CAUSED 2026-10-01: it is not per-robot detection

The operator asked the right question -- how can vision be "lost" at 30+
pose updates per robot per second? It is not. Three findings:

**1. "Lost vision" means the NAVIGATOR'S CALLBACK has not fired for 500ms**
(`VISION_STALE_SEC`), because `pose.t` is stamped on receipt, not on
capture. It says nothing about whether the camera saw the tag.

**2. The stalls are FLEET-WIDE.** All six events in the run fall into two
clusters:

```
1790794397.20  alvik1, alvik6, alvik3, alvik1   span 1.33s
1790794407.93  alvik5, alvik3                   span 0.61s
```

Robots in different parts of the table do not lose their own tags within
1.33s of each other. The whole pose pipeline stopped.

**3. The reported rate CANNOT show this.** The vision terminal averages over
a 5s window, so a 0.6s hole -- more than enough to fail every robot
mid-turn -- still reads 42.2 Hz:

| gap | msgs lost | reported rate |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5s | 24 | 43.2 Hz |
| 0.6s | 29 | 42.2 Hz |
| 1.0s | 48 | 38.4 Hz |

You would have to lose 1.5s before the headline number looked wrong.

**Ruled out:** the pose-jump filter (silently discards poses that move too
far) is not doing this -- it allows 2160deg/s of yaw at 48Hz against a
measured max of 141deg/s, and 3.42in of position against ~0.07in actual.

**Likely source, with precedent:** the camera relay. On 2026-09-30 the
vision terminal showed three windows at 75/81/122 poses/sec with read
medians of 58/55ms and loop rates of 12.9/13.5/20.4 Hz -- with the robots
switched OFF, so it is not fleet load.
